# NVIDIA: Strong Operating Performance, Valuation Still Required

**Company:** NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA)  
**Report date:** September 3, 2026  
**Valuation date:** February 25, 2026  
**Decision user:** A fund investment committee  
**Current call:** **Watch-defer**

## Investment decision

The decision is whether the fund should initiate a position in NVIDIA, continue monitoring it, or decline the investment. My call remains **watch-defer**. NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 results show exceptional growth, profitability, and operating cash generation, but this report does not yet contain the valuation-date share price, enterprise value, comparable-company multiples, or a discounted cash-flow valuation. **[Author judgment]**

A strong business is not automatically an attractive investment at every price. I would defer initiation until a price-based valuation demonstrates an adequate expected return and margin of safety under reasonable downside assumptions. **[Author judgment]**

## What the business does

NVIDIA began with PC graphics but now describes itself as a data-center-scale AI infrastructure company. Its GPU-based platforms support accelerated computing, artificial intelligence, scientific computing, data science, gaming, autonomous vehicles, robotics, and digital-twin applications. The company reports two operating segments: Compute & Networking and Graphics. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

In plain language, NVIDIA sells the computing and networking technology used to build and operate demanding AI and graphics systems. Compute & Networking includes its Data Center platforms, while Graphics includes products serving gaming and professional visualization. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 36 and 39–40; Note 16, pp. 76–78]**

The business is now heavily dependent on Data Center. Fiscal 2026 Data Center revenue was $193.737 billion compared with total revenue of $215.938 billion. Data Center therefore represented approximately 89.7% of total revenue. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Note 16, p. 78; author calculation: $193.737 billion / $215.938 billion]**

## What changed in fiscal 2026

Fiscal 2026 revenue increased 65% to $215.938 billion from $130.497 billion. Management attributed the increase primarily to Data Center compute and networking platforms used for accelerated computing and AI, with Blackwell architectures representing most Data Center revenue. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 36–37]**

Within Data Center, compute revenue increased 59% because of demand for the Blackwell computing platform. Networking revenue increased 142% as NVIDIA introduced and expanded NVLink compute fabric for GB200 and GB300 systems and grew its Ethernet and InfiniBand platforms. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 40]**

Growth was not limited to Data Center. Gaming revenue increased 41%, Professional Visualization revenue increased 70%, and Automotive revenue increased 39%. Management connected those changes to Blackwell demand, the DGX Spark launch, and continued adoption of self-driving platforms. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 37]**

Profitability increased in dollars but weakened as a percentage of revenue. Operating income rose 60% to $130.387 billion, while gross margin declined from 75.0% to 71.1%. Management attributed the margin decline to the transition from Hopper HGX systems to full-scale Blackwell data-center solutions and a $4.5 billion charge for H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations following U.S. export restrictions. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 36–37 and 40]**

Operating expenses increased 41% to $23.076 billion. Research and development expense rose because of employee growth, compensation increases, and a 79% increase in compute and infrastructure costs. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 37 and 40–41]**

Operating cash flow increased to $102.718 billion from $64.089 billion, which management attributed to higher revenue. At fiscal year-end, NVIDIA reported $10.605 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $51.951 billion of marketable securities. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 42]**

Using the filing amounts, cash plus marketable securities less the $8.468 billion net carrying amount of debt equals approximately $54.088 billion of net financial liquidity. This is an analytical calculation rather than a measure reported by NVIDIA. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Consolidated Balance Sheets, p. 53; Notes 7 and 11, pp. 64–65 and 69; author calculation]**

## What looks favorable

The favorable part of the initial thesis is the scale of NVIDIA's operating momentum. Revenue and net income each increased 65%, diluted earnings per share increased 67% to $4.90, and operating cash flow exceeded $100 billion. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 37 and 42]**

Management is also introducing new Data Center architectures on a one-year cadence. It began shipping production units of Blackwell Ultra, including GB300, during fiscal 2026 and identified Rubin as a future platform. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

## What could go wrong

The first risk is that customer demand depends on resources outside NVIDIA's direct control. Management says customers and partners need suitable data centers, energy, and capital to deploy NVIDIA AI infrastructure. Shortages or financing constraints could delay deployments or reduce their scale. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

Rapid product transitions create execution risk. NVIDIA warns that complex configurations and transitions can cause production delays, quality problems, lower yields, higher material and warranty costs, excess inventory, and revenue volatility. Customers may also delay purchases while moving to a new architecture. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

Regulation has already produced a measurable cost. U.S. licensing requirements reduced demand for the H20 product in China and contributed to the $4.5 billion charge. NVIDIA generated only about $60 million of H20 revenue under licenses granted in August 2025. As of the filing, it had generated no H200 licensing-program revenue and did not know whether China would permit imports. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

Revenue is concentrated among a small number of customers. One direct customer represented 22% of fiscal 2026 revenue and another represented 14%, primarily in Compute & Networking. Management also says a limited number of indirect customers each may represent 10% or more of revenue. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 40]**

NVIDIA's supply decisions depend on forecasts made well before final demand is known. Manufacturing lead times can exceed twelve months, and the company may place non-cancellable orders, pay premiums, or provide deposits to secure capacity. Inventory and excess-purchase-obligation provisions totaled $7.2 billion in fiscal 2026. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 37–38 and 40]**

NVIDIA also warns that open-source AI could reduce demand if developers deploy models on competitors' platforms. The filing does not quantify the probability or potential financial effect of this risk. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36]**

## Consequential assumptions and open questions

The watch-defer call depends on several assumptions that still require testing:

- Near-term AI infrastructure demand was strong as of the valuation date. Alphabet expected to invest $175–$185 billion in 2026 capital expenditures and reported a $240 billion Cloud backlog driven partly by AI demand. Microsoft likewise reported that demand exceeded available capacity after spending $37.5 billion in quarterly capital expenditures, approximately two-thirds of which went toward GPUs and CPUs. Whether this demand remains durable over the longer term is still an author assumption. **[Alphabet Q4 2025 earnings call, February 4, 2026; Microsoft FY2026 Q2 earnings call, January 28, 2026]**

- NVIDIA's fiscal 2026 results demonstrate strong historical margins and operating cash flow, but they do not prove that future Data Center growth will remain equally profitable. Microsoft reported that AI infrastructure investment pressured its cloud gross margin and that higher cash capital expenditures reduced sequential free cash flow. Continued attractive NVIDIA margins and cash generation therefore remain an author assumption. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 37 and 40–42; Microsoft FY2026 Q2 earnings call, January 28, 2026]**

- NVIDIA's competitive advantage is not guaranteed. Alphabet said it uses NVIDIA GPUs alongside internally developed TPUs, while Microsoft discussed its own Maia accelerator and its use of NVIDIA and AMD technology. AMD also reported that its 2025 Data Center revenue increased 32% to $16.6 billion. NVIDIA maintaining its advantage therefore remains an author assumption that must be monitored. **[Alphabet Q4 2025 earnings call, February 4, 2026; Microsoft FY2026 Q2 earnings call, January 28, 2026; AMD FY2025 results, February 3, 2026]**

- Microsoft stated that many GPUs it purchases are contracted to customers for most or all of their useful lives. This provides indirect evidence of committed industry demand, but it does not prove that NVIDIA's own supply and capacity commitments will become profitable sales. **[Microsoft FY2026 Q2 earnings call, January 28, 2026; NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 37–38; author assumption]**

- Export restrictions already affected NVIDIA's H20 sales and inventory. Whether future restrictions will remove a materially larger portion of NVIDIA's addressable market remains uncertain. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 36; author assumption]**

- NVIDIA's customer concentration is verified, but its future effect on pricing and revenue cannot yet be determined. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 40; author assumption]**

## Recent Company News and CEO Perspective

On January 5, 2026, NVIDIA announced its six-chip Rubin AI platform. The company stated that Rubin was in production, with partner products expected in the second half of 2026, and identified AWS, Google, Microsoft, Meta, OpenAI, and other organizations as expected adopters. **[NVIDIA Newsroom, January 5, 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/rubin-platform-ai-supercomputer)**

On February 25, 2026, NVIDIA reported quarterly revenue of $68.1 billion, up 73% year over year, and quarterly Data Center revenue of $62.3 billion, up 75%. The company projected first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of $78.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, while assuming no Data Center compute revenue from China. **[NVIDIA Investor Relations, February 25, 2026](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-2026/)**

During the February 25 earnings call, management said Blackwell demand continued to strengthen, expected sequential revenue growth throughout calendar 2026, and stated that inventory and supply commitments were in place for shipments extending into 2027. CEO Jensen Huang argued that growth in agentic AI would create sustained demand for computing capacity. These comments support the near-term demand thesis, but they are management forecasts rather than independently verified future outcomes. **[NVIDIA Q4 FY2026 earnings call, February 25, 2026, pp. 3–6 and 16–17](https://s201.q4cdn.com/141608511/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/NVDA-Q4-2026-Earnings-Call-25-February-2026-5_00-PM-ET.pdf)**

## Research needed before initiation

The next step is to obtain the February 25, 2026 share price from a dated market source and reconcile shares outstanding, equity awards, cash, investments, and debt to calculate equity value and enterprise value. I would then build a five-year free-cash-flow valuation with explicit downside, base, and upside assumptions. **[Author research plan]**

I would also compare the latest filing with NVIDIA's prior two 10-Ks, review dated investor-relations releases and an earnings call or executive interview, and test whether management's earlier expectations were achieved. Every added fact should be cited where it appears in the report; unsupported assertions should remain marked `UNSOURCED` or be removed. **[Author research plan]**

## Conclusion

My call remains **watch-defer**. NVIDIA reported extraordinary fiscal 2026 growth and cash generation, led by Blackwell and Data Center demand, but the filing also identifies lower gross margin, export-control costs, customer concentration, infrastructure constraints, and product-transition risk. **[NVIDIA FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 36–42]**

I would change the call to **initiate-buy** if a price-based valuation shows an attractive expected return and adequate margin of safety under reasonable downside assumptions. **[Author judgment]**
